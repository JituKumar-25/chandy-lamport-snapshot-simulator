How the algorithm works

  1. The initiator records its local state.
  2. The initiator sends a marker on every outgoing channel.
  3. A process receiving its first marker records its local state and marks that channel empty.
  4. That process forwards markers and starts recording its other incoming channels.
  5. A later marker stops recording on its channel; the recorded messages are that channel's state.
  6. Process states plus channel states combine into the global snapshot.

Learning mode

Turn on Learning mode to see a reason under every event and the current step.

Why is the first marker special? It tells a process the snapshot has started, so it must save its state before it handles anything else.

What is an in-transit message? A message sent before the sender's snapshot but received after the receiver's snapshot. It is stored in the channel state.

Why is the result consistent? No recorded process has received a message that its sender had not yet sent.
